# CryptoGuru — Acquisition Overview & Due-Diligence Guide

> Prepared for prospective buyers evaluating the CryptoGuru software project. Commercial terms, included assets and transfer scope must be agreed separately in a written transaction.

## 1. What is being offered

CryptoGuru is a Telegram-oriented cryptocurrency application combining:
- A Telegram bot and Mini App.
- DEMO / Grid virtual trading.
- A virtual Trading Engine with accounting and position simulation.
- AI PAPER decision-generation development.
- AI Companion functionality.
- Backend services and persistent application state.
- External market-data and Walt integration groundwork.
- GitHub Actions-based engineering automation.
- Tests, documentation and supporting project assets.

The public showcase describes the product without exposing the private source repository.

## 3. Product maturity and limitations

The project has substantial implemented code, particularly around the application backend, virtual trading and user-facing features. Its subsystems are at different maturity levels.

Important limitations:
- DEMO/Grid and the Trading Engine are virtual-trading components.
- AI PAPER still requires lifecycle, accounting and risk-control work.
- LIVE/Walt is not ready for real-money trading.
- Worker automation has workflow reliability and configuration items to resolve.
- Current deployment, user activity, revenue, uptime and trading performance have not been independently established by this showcase.

The [Feature Status](feature-status.md) document provides the engineering snapshot and open work.

## 4. What a transaction may include

Subject to verification, agreement and rights review, a transfer package may include:

The exact inventory must be written into the purchase agreement. Do not assume that external accounts, provider subscriptions, credentials, third-party services or third-party intellectual property are included.

## 6. Buyer due-diligence checklist

### Technical
- [ ] Review the exact source commit offered for transfer.
- [ ] Inspect architecture, dependencies and build process.
- [ ] Run the full test suite and verify CI results.

- [ ] Reproduce the main product demonstration.

### Security and operations
- [ ] Review secret handling and deployment configuration.
- [ ] Inspect GitHub Actions permissions and automation.
- [ ] Review authentication, authorization and session handling.
- [ ] Assess risk controls and accounting logic.
- [ ] Confirm current deployment state and operational costs.
- [ ] Agree a credential rotation and access-transfer plan.

### Commercial and legal
- [ ] Confirm the included asset inventory.
- [ ] Review third-party licenses and transfer restrictions.
- [ ] Confirm ownership and contributor rights.
- [ ] Confirm whether domains, brands, social accounts or bot identities are included.
- [ ] Agree warranties, exclusions, support period and acceptance criteria.
- [ ] Execute a written purchase or assignment agreement.

## 7. Suggested transaction process

1. **Initial review** — buyer reviews the public showcase and submits questions.
2. **Qualification** — parties agree confidentiality and the scope of private technical access.
3. **Technical due diligence** — buyer reviews a pinned commit, tests and a controlled demonstration.
4. **Asset and rights review** — parties confirm the exact transfer inventory and third-party exclusions.
5. **Agreement** — parties sign written terms covering price, rights, liabilities, handover and acceptance.
6. **Transfer** — repository and agreed assets are transferred using a controlled process.
7. **Credential rotation** — credentials are rotated; access and ownership are updated.
8. **Acceptance and handover** — buyer validates the delivered package against agreed criteria.

## 8. Exclusions unless expressly agreed

Unless a signed agreement states otherwise, the following should not be assumed to be included:
- Cloudflare, Telegram, Gemini, market-data or Walt accounts.
- API keys, OAuth secrets, bot tokens or other credentials.
- Third-party software or assets beyond the rights granted by their licenses.
- Guaranteed revenue, users, trading returns or profitability.
- Regulatory approval, exchange authorization or financial-services licenses.
- Ongoing hosting, maintenance or support beyond the agreed transition period.

## 9. Valuation considerations

A credible valuation should consider:
- Replacement cost and engineering effort.
- Quality and maintainability of the codebase.
- Demonstrated functionality and reproducibility.
- Deployment readiness and infrastructure costs.
- Security and technical debt.
- Transferable intellectual-property scope.
- Verified users, revenue, retention or other commercial traction, if any.
- Remaining work required to reach the buyer's intended use case.

No revenue, paying customers, verified trading profits or production uptime are claimed in this public document. Any asking price should be treated as a seller proposal, not an independent valuation.
